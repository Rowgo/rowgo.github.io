Tipsy Turtles

The Night Crew and I made Tipsy Turtles for our final project at Vancouver Film School. For this game I was resposible for designing and implimenting the UI/UX for the game, managing the project, creating the visual effects, and documenting the game's core mechanics.

Fish Bowl UI

The core part of our game was in the obtuse movement controls that you and your friend had to overcome to progress. However we felt that by itself the game would simply be about moving slowly to not make mistakes, but games with obtuse controls are often fun because you make silly mistakes. So to pressure players into making mistakes we added a time element to the game which also served as the narrative goal for the characters. Save a fish by bringing it to cleaner water, but don't let the leaky fishbowl empty before the next checkpoint.

I was tasked with designing and implimenting the UI and gameplay for both the fishbowl and check points. I decided to make the UI diagetic despite my better judgement. Since the fishbowl was going to be an object that was constantly in the players veiw port making the UI diagetic would prevent us from duplicating work and would allow us to put more resources into making the fishbowl look good as it could. This was important since the fishbowl was a core to our gameplay and narrative.

After creating the A-Z of our game we realized that to give a better veiw of the fish bowl we would need to place the camera directly behind the players instead of slightly overhead like we had planned. I discussed this with the team and we decided to go forward with moving the camera, much to the dismay of our enviorment artist. (sorry Jaylon).
For the checkpoints that would refill the fishbowl I used outdoor faucets that you might find in a backyard or a park. However an issue I quickly ran into though was that the faucets refill volume made the refilling the bowl too hard and it couldn't be made wider without making the faucet look proportionally absurd, so to allow the refill volume's size to be dictaded by gameplay and not art I designed the water to look like a leak springing from the pipe as a result of entropy.

During the begining of the project players rarely noticed the fishbowl, and while it required a lot of work like changing the position of the camera moving the fishbowl onto the skateboard and off the turtles' shell, and giving the fish dramatic animations when the water got low, we eventually got to a point where players consistently noticed it in our blind playtests. While there are obviously many things that could be improved about the fishbowl's UI I'm happy with the decision of making it diagetic. it gave us the oppurtuinty to inject a lot of personality into the fish's character and have players connect with the game more as a result.
