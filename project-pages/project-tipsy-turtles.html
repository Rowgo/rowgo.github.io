Tipsy Turtles

The Night Crew and I made Tipsy Turtles for our final project at Vancouver Film School. For this game I was responsible for designing and implementing the UI/UX for the game, managing the project, creating the visual effects, and documenting the game's core mechanics.

Fish Bowl UI

The core of our game was in the obtuse movement controls that the players had to overcome together to progress. However we felt that by itself the game would simply be about moving slowly to prevent mistakes, but games with obtuse controls are often fun because of those silly mistakes. So to pressure players into making mistakes we added a time element to the game which also served as the narrative goal for the characters.
Save a fish by bringing it to cleaner water, but don't let the leaky fishbowl empty before the next checkpoint.

I was tasked with designing and implementing the UI and gameplay for both the fishbowl and check points. I decided to make the UI diegetic, despite my better judgement. Since the fishbowl was going to be an object that was constantly in the players view port making the UI diegetic would prevent us from duplicating work and would allow us to put more resources into making the fishbowl look as good as it could. This was important since the fishbowl was a core to our gameplay, and narrative.

After creating the A-Z of our game we realized that to give a better view of the fish bowl we would need to place the camera directly behind the players, instead of slightly overhead like we had originally planned. I discussed this with the team, and we decided to go forward with moving the camera. Much to the dismay of our environment artist, (sorry Jaylon).
For the checkpoints that would refill the fishbowl I used outdoor faucets that you might find in a backyard or a park. However an issue I quickly ran into though was that the stream from the faucet visually didn't match how big we needed the refill volume to be. If we made it bigger the faucet or the stream would end up looking proportionally absurd, and if we made the refill volume to small the game wasn't forgiving enough. so to allow the refill volume's size to be dictated by gameplay and not art I designed the water to look like a leak springing from the pipe as a result of entropy.

During the beginning of the project players rarely noticed the fishbowl, and while it required a lot of work like changing the position of the camera, moving the fishbowl onto the skateboard and off the turtles' shell, and giving the fish dramatic animations when the water got low, we eventually got to a point where players consistently noticed it in our blind playtests. While there are obviously many things that could be improved about the fishbowl's UI I'm happy with the decision of making it diegetic. It gave us the opportunity to inject a lot of personality into the fish's character and have players connect with the game more as a result.
